## Contributing work you don't solely own

If any part of your contribution was written by someone else, or you don't hold
the copyright in all of it, don't submit it as your own. Open an issue describing
the situation first — including where the code came from and under what licence —
and we'll work out whether and how it can be included.

If you wrote it for an employer, they may own it. In that case your employer needs
to sign the Entity agreement ([`ha-cla-e-v1.pdf`](ha-cla-e-v1.pdf)).

## What this means, in plain language

This summary is for orientation only. Where it and the agreement text above
disagree, the agreement text governs.

**You keep your copyright** (§2.1a). You are not giving away ownership. You can
reuse your own contribution anywhere else, including commercially, without asking
anyone.

**You let Dave license your contribution under other terms, including proprietary
ones** (§2.3). Dave is GPL-3.0-or-later today and is intended to stay open source,
but the project deliberately preserves the ability to offer a commercial licence
alongside it. This permission can't practically be added after the fact, which is
why it's asked for up front.

**In exchange, your work is guaranteed to stay open** (§2.3, second sentence). If
Dave ships your contribution under a proprietary licence, it must *also* keep
licensing it under the project's licence as of the day you submitted it. A
proprietary Dave cannot mean a Dave your contribution disappears from.

**You grant a patent licence** (§2.2), so the project can't later be attacked with
a patent claim over code that was contributed freely.

**You confirm you're allowed to contribute it** (§3). Your own work, or work you
have the rights to submit.

We chose to state the relicensing permission plainly rather than leave it to be
inferred from a broad sublicensing clause. If you'd rather not grant it, please
don't submit code — but issues, bug reports, reproductions, testing and
documentation feedback are all welcome and need no agreement at all.
